SIH26242 · minor project · Tanishq Khandelwal and team

Hunar, every screen on one page.

Hunar is an offline first PWA that helps assess electricians who learned on the job. A facilitator records what the worker says they can do, the app maps it to official NSQF qualification packs, the assessor scores six practical tasks against an anchored rubric, and only the assessor's sign off produces a recommendation. Every screen below is rebuilt from frontend/src with the app's own styles and English strings.

Screens use the repo's SAMPLE worker. Scores, percentages and audit rows inside the frames are placeholders to show layout, not project results. The repo's real results live in experiments/*.json.

05

Assessor scoring

/a/:id/scoreAssessor default tabM3

Two steps. Confirm the pack, then decide all 28 criteria across six tasks, each with an explained pre score.

  • Picking any pack other than the top suggestion needs a written reason.
  • The engine suggests 0 to 2 only. Level 3 is always an assessor call.
  • Accept is switched off while checklist or photo changes are unsynced. Override needs a level and a reason of 5+ characters.
  • Safety criteria carry a red stripe. A checklist and photo conflict is flagged in red.

Read of the codebase

What holds up, and what to fix before a real pilot

Strong

  • The ethics are in the UI, not only the README. Reasons are mandatory for overrides and non top packs, safety criteria hard block, SAMPLE badges follow sample data everywhere, wording never says “certified”.
  • Offline first is real. Every action except sign off goes to an IndexedDB outbox and replays in order, with a visible queue and a clear failure path.
  • Built for the field. 48px tap targets, Yes / No buttons instead of tiny checkboxes, full Hindi strings, readable at 360px.

Gaps

  • No login. Anyone can pick Admin or Assessor and type any name, and that name goes into the “tamper evident” log. The chain proves rows weren't edited, not who wrote them.
  • The worker never sees anything. There is no worker facing result or receipt. The person being assessed is absent from the app.
  • Voice is the missing front door. The target user talks better than they type, and the mic button is disabled.
  • Scoring is one long scroll of 28 cards with no per task progress or jump list, and level descriptors stay English in Hindi mode.
  • Two screens run empty today. The PPE detector reports “not available” and rater mode has only sample items until YOLO and M4 have real results.
